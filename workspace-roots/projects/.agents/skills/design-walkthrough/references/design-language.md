# Visual and Interaction Design Language

Use precise terms to make feedback actionable. Describe what is observable
before explaining why it may matter.

## Product structure

- **User outcome:** result the user is trying to achieve.
- **Mental model:** the user's working explanation of what the product contains
  and how it behaves.
- **Information architecture:** organization, naming, grouping, and routes
  through information.
- **Object model:** kinds of things the product contains and their relationships.
- **Schema:** fields consistently shown for every instance of an object.
- **Navigation:** movement between places or object views.
- **Progressive disclosure:** delay detail until it becomes relevant without
  hiding required information.

## Visual composition

- **Hierarchy:** order of perceived importance.
- **Composition:** placement and relationship of elements in the whole frame.
- **Grouping:** visual signals that elements belong together.
- **Rhythm:** repeated spacing, type, and shape that create a predictable scan.
- **Density:** amount of information within an area.
- **Emphasis:** contrast used to direct attention.
- **Typography:** typeface, size, weight, line length, and spacing as a system.
- **Alignment:** shared edges or axes that make relationships legible.

## Interaction

- **Affordance:** action an object makes possible.
- **Signifier:** visible cue communicating that action.
- **Mapping:** relationship between a control and its effect.
- **Feedback:** visible response confirming system state after an action.
- **State:** current condition of an object or process.
- **Consistency:** same meaning and behavior across repeated patterns.
- **Recovery:** route out of error, confusion, or unintended action.
- **Constraint:** limitation that prevents invalid or harmful action.

## Content and meaning

- **Label:** shortest name for an object or action.
- **Microcopy:** small instructional, status, or explanatory text.
- **CTA:** action label that states what happens next.
- **Metadata:** supporting facts presented consistently for an object.
- **Rationale:** explanation for a design decision; review material unless the
  user needs it to use the product.
- **Annotation:** commentary about the design; never product UI.

## AI interaction

- **Capability boundary:** what the system can and cannot actually do.
- **Confidence:** calibrated strength of an output, not decorative certainty.
- **Provenance:** source and transformation path behind an answer.
- **Contestability:** user's ability to question, correct, or reject an output.
- **Control:** user's ability to direct, stop, undo, or change system behavior.
- **Learning contract:** what feedback is used, how, and when its effect appears.
- **Attention item:** a verified consequential outcome or rare concrete
  human-authority request promoted into the user's awareness. Attention is a
  presentation projection, not task state; possibility alone does not qualify.
- **Authority request:** a rare, concrete decision that automation cannot own.
  Name the authority being exercised; any resulting commitment belongs to its
  owning work system.
- **Obligation copy:** contextual prose may clarify that the user owes nothing
  when useful. It is not a mandatory label, status, field, or classification.

## Finding language

Prefer:

> On the home screen, the three captured items use two CTA labels without a
> visible state difference. Joel could not predict why one said Open and another
> said Discuss. This is an observed consistency and mapping problem.

Avoid:

> The buttons feel inconsistent and should probably be improved.

Never use polish as a substitute for clarity. A visually refined screen can
still have a broken object model, inconsistent semantics, or an inaccurate
mental model.
